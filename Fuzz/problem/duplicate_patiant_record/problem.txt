Why I built it this way — the traps are intentional:

Case	IDs	Why it's tricky
Clear duplicates (typos, spacing, case)	1-5, 8-10, 12-14, 17-18	Should merge — this is the "easy" fuzzy match win
Similar name, different person	6-7, 11	High name similarity but should NOT merge — this is where phone number as a secondary check saves you
Same name, different phone	15-16	The hardest case — is this one person with 2 numbers, or 2 different people who happen to share a name? Your program needs to decide a rule here (e.g., "same name + same age → likely same person even with phone mismatch" or flag it for manual review instead of auto-deciding)
Suggested approach to test against this data:

Fuzzy-match on name alone first — see how many false merges you get (6, 7, 11 should NOT merge but might with too loose a threshold).
Add phone as a hard secondary signal — same phone + similar name = high confidence merge.
Decide what to do with case 15-16 explicitly, don't let it fall through silently.

Key design decisions baked into this ground truth:

Similar name + different phone → do NOT auto-merge. Name-only similarity is too risky (cases 6, 7, 11 prove this).
